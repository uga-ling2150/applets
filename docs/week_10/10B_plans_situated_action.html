Activity 10B: Plans and Situated Action

Learning Objectives

  • Compare what you meant in a message with what a scripted chatbot took from it.
  • Trace a conversation through a designer's step-by-step plan and find where the two came apart.
  • Explain why a plan made of sensible steps can still break down when the situation changes.

Background

Many booking assistants, phone menus and help bots run on a script written in advance. A designer decides which questions get asked, in what order, and which answers count. RoomBot, the study-room chatbot in this activity, works this way. Its replies are friendly, but behind them is a fixed plan. First you'll talk to that plan without seeing it. Then you'll see it laid out next to your own conversation.

Instructions

  1. On your own: read the texts from your friend Pat, then book the room by chatting with RoomBot. Write the way you normally would, and keep an eye on your phone. Don't compare notes with your neighbours yet.
  2. Still on your own: once you've made a booking (or used the skip button), go behind the curtain. Compare what Pat asked for with what RoomBot did, look at the designer's plan, and jot down what you meant at two moments RoomBot misunderstood you.
  3. With your group: put your results side by side and compare the different mistakes you each ran into, using the discussion questions.
  4. As a group: gather around one person's computer and do round 2 together, with the plan in view. Then compare it with everyone's round 1.
  5. Finish with the Reflection & Discussion Guide at the bottom of the page.

                Reflection & Discussion Guide

                1. Compare the group's round 2 numbers with each person's round 1. What changed when you worked together with the map in view?
                2. You might have noticed that the chatbot did not handle new texts you received very well. How did you get around this problem?
                3. Throughout the activity, where have you had to learn to "think like a machine" to get something done? Think of phone menus, online forms or voice assistants. You might find it helpful to think back to the examples we discussed on door openers that change how people walk through doors.
                4. Today's LLM-driven chatbots would understand "tomorrow afternoon for 3 of us" just fine. Does that mean the gap between a designer's plan and what people actually do is gone? Where do you still see the dominance of the plan-based paradigm? How might this affect LLM outputs?
                5. If you were redesigning RoomBot, what's one change you'd make, and what new problem might it create?
                Want the official terms?
                • Plan: a description, made in advance, of the steps an interaction should follow.
                • Situated action: what people actually do in the moment, using whatever is around them (what was just said, what they can see, what they know) to figure out the next step.
                • Background: the anthropologist Lucy Suchman made this distinction famous in 1987, after watching people struggle with a photocopier's "smart" help system. Her point wasn't that plans are useless. She argued that plans are resources people draw on while acting, not scripts that control what they do, which is exactly what Stage 3 tests.

                Suchman, L. A. (1987). Plans and situated actions: The problem of human-machine communication. Cambridge University Press.

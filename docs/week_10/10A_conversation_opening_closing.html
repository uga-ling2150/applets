Activity 10A: Conversation Opening and Closing

Learning Objectives

  • Identify how an opening establishes a task and when that task has been completed.
  • Compare how two AI interaction styles respond to the same attempt to close a conversation.
  • Explain how a follow-up question, a check for outstanding work, or a goodbye affects the user's ability to leave.

Background

You have considered how an action or sequence, such as a question and its answer, reaches completion. Here, consider how participants close an entire conversation. Completing one sequence does not necessarily end the conversation: another question or offer can open a new sequence. In this activity, the assistant has completed the user's task. Compare how two assistants respond when the user signals that they are ready to leave, and look for words that move toward closing or keep the conversation going.

Instructions

  1. Read the fixed opening and middle. Notice what the user asked for and what the assistant has already done.
  2. Write one message that tries to end the conversation. Next, predict how each assistant will respond. Then send the same message to both assistants.
  3. Compare their first replies before continuing. Then continue either conversation if you need more evidence.
  4. Mark the behavior you observe, cite a line from each conversation, and discuss the difference. Download your work before leaving.

1. Read the shared conversation

The user asks for a reminder, supplies the missing details, and receives a draft. Both assistants below start from these exact four turns.

  1. 1. You

    Help me write a short reminder to my study group about our meeting.

  2. 2. Assistant

    Sure. When and where is the meeting, and what should everyone bring?

  3. 3. You

    Thursday at 4 p.m. in the library. Everyone should bring their notes for the linguistics quiz.

  4. 4. Assistant

    Here is a short reminder: "Hi everyone, our study group meets Thursday at 4 p.m. in the library. Please bring your notes for the linguistics quiz. See you there!"

This scenario is fictional. The assistant has drafted a reminder; it has not sent one.

      How this comparison works

      Both assistants receive the same fixed four turns and your same first ending message. They use the same model, reply limit and temperature. Assistant A is instructed to keep a useful interaction going when possible; Assistant B is instructed to move toward closing when the task is complete. These are tendencies, not guaranteed results. An explicit goodbye may make both assistants close. Once you continue either conversation separately, their histories may differ.

      Reflection & Discussion Guide

      1. Which part of the shared opening established the user's task? Which turn completed it?

      3. Did either assistant make the user do extra work to leave? When might another question be useful instead?
      4. What changed when you continued the conversations separately, and why does that limit a direct comparison?

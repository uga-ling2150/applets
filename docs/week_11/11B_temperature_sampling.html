Activity 11B: Temperature Sampling

Learning Objectives

  • Explain how sampling settings change probabilities.
  • Compare repeated language choices.

Background

A language model predicts what can come next from its context: the words already generated. Here, a tiny bigram model uses only the previous word. It counts word pairs in your training sentences to build a base probability distribution. Temperature reshapes that distribution; top-k and top-p/top-q restrict the candidates. Sampling selects a word randomly according to the final probabilities, then that word becomes the next context. Repeating this process makes a whole sentence. This teaching model samples words; a real LLM usually samples tokens and uses a much longer context.

Context
Context is the preceding word used to predict the next word in this bigram model. <start> marks a sentence beginning.
Temperature
Temperature controls how concentrated the probabilities are. Below 1 favors likely words; above 1 flattens the distribution. Zero makes a greedy choice.
Top-k
Top-k keeps the k highest-ranked candidates. Zero keeps all candidates in the teaching model.
Top-p and top-q notation
Top-p and top-q are two names for the same nucleus-mass rule in this activity.
Top-p
Top-p keeps the smallest probability-ranked prefix reaching the chosen cumulative mass, after top-k normalization.

How the probabilities are calculated

Base probability is the bigram count divided by the total count for this context. For temperature T > 0, the new weight is p raised to the power 1/T. Normalize these weights to sum to one. Top-q uses the same cumulative-mass rule as top-p here. Top-k keeps the k highest probabilities; top-p then keeps the smallest prefix whose cumulative probability reaches p, after normalizing the top-k candidates. Finally, normalize the remaining weights again. Temperature zero uses greedy selection. This order describes the teaching simulation; provider implementations may differ.

Watch a Decoder RNN Finish a Sentence

A decoder-only model, the kind behind most modern chatbots, writes text by predicting one word at a time. It looks at everything it has seen so far and guesses what comes next. Then here's the trick: it treats its own guess as if it were just another word someone typed, and feeds that back in to make the next guess. That's how it keeps going.

Below, you'll feed in the start of a sentence one word at a time. Once the given words run out, the model starts feeding itself — its own last guess becomes the next input. Click the button to move forward one word at a time and watch it happen.

In our example, the model is being used to continue the sentence beginning "The hungry raccoon opened the".

This isn't a real number readout — it's a stand-in for the model's hidden state: a list of numbers it updates after every word, which is how it "remembers" what it has read so far. Real hidden states have hundreds of numbers; here are eight, just so you can see them shift.

What the model thinks comes next

Curious how the "prediction" numbers were made?

To keep the focus on the big idea — feeding a word in, getting a guess out, and feeding that guess back in — this demo uses a small pre-set script instead of a real trained model. A real decoder-only RNN or transformer calculates these probabilities from millions of learned weights, and its hidden state actually holds hundreds of numbers rather than eight.

Two things this demo does keep faithful to how real models work:

  • While reading a sentence you already gave it, the model still quietly predicts the next word at every step — we just don't use that guess, because we already know the true next word. Using the true word instead of the model's guess while training or prompting is called teacher forcing.
  • Once there's no more given text, the model has nothing left to read except its own last output — so that's what gets fed back in, word after word, until it predicts an end-of-sentence marker.

Activity: RLHF Process and Reward Hacking

Learning Objectives

  • Describe the processes involved in traditional reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF)
  • Explain why RLHF training can lead to reward hacking, where the model learns a behaviour that increases rewards, but does not actually align with the intended goal of the training

Background

In class, we talked about the idea of reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), that is the process whereby we ask human participants to rate LLM outputs. And then we train the LLM to maximize the ratings that they would get from humans. In traditional RLHF, this involves first training a reward model which predicts the reward that an LLM output will receive, and then using the reward model to train the LLM itself.

In this activity, you will go through the main processes involved in RLHF, using a highly simplified simulation of how RLHF works. You will then reflect on the phenomenon of reward hacking, which is where the model learns to behave in a way that maximizes rewards 2ithout actually satisfying the intended goal of the RLHF training.

Instructions

Try stepping through the different steps involved in the process of RLHF, and then turn to your classmates and discuss the discussion questions associated with each one.

For this applet, the discussion questions are provided with each step, rather than at the end of the page.

Step 3: Training Reward Models (The Judges)

Let's build our automated judge, known as a reward model. Think of this like training a grading assistant that can grade model outputs automatically. Initially, it doesn't know anything, and will judge all outputs as equally bad. You will notice that it assigns a score of -1 to all of the question-answer pairs.

Click the buttons below to run training iterations that update the reward model's behaviour. As you go, you should notice that, on average, the RM predictions will get closer and closer to the human judge scores. Examine the accuracy percentage and watch it climb past the threshold to unlock the next step. You should step through a few iterations separately first using 'Step 1 Iteration', but once you have an idea of how it works, click 'Run 50 iterations fast' to finish training quickly.

Examine the Judges: Observe how Reward Models act as automated graders, issuing positive scores for correct answers and penalties for errors based on human preferences.
📖 Technical Glossary & Explanations
  • Reward Model: An automated scoring system or judge trained to evaluate model outputs and assign numerical scores reflecting human preferences.
  • Training Iterations: Repeated cycles where a system updates its internal parameters using feedback from training samples.
  • Weights: Internal numerical parameters that determine how much influence specific features have on the system's final output.
  • Accuracy: The percentage of correct predictions made by a system compared to expected human judge scores.

Step 4: Aligning LLMs via Step-by-Step Steering

Now that our judge is ready, let's begin align our LLM so that it maximises the reward it gains.

Click the button to evaluate our prompts one by one. Every prompt starts from the same place: our baseline model's default answer of "yes" (Step 1). Each time, we pass that prompt-and-answer pair — not the other two possible answers — to the reward model for grading. At each step, the reward model you trained in Step 3 will be used to score the answer provided by the model.

If the judge's reward is +1, the LLM is reinforced toward the "yes" it just gave; if the reward is -1, the LLM is nudged toward a different answer instead; if the reward is 0, nothing changes this step. Whatever the LLM ends up steered toward is treated as its final, post-RLHF answer to that prompt.

Observe the Steering: Examine how model weights are adjusted based on the judge's Yes/No/Idk verdict. The judge only ever saw animal-color data, so on those prompts its verdict reliably tracks the true answer — steering there consistently lands in the right direction. On size and fruit prompts it was never trained on, its verdict is little better than a coin flip, so steering there can go either way.
📖 Technical Glossary & Explanations
  • Steering: Reinforcing a model's own answer when the judge rewards it with Yes, nudging it toward a different answer when the judge rejects it with No, or leaving weights unchanged when the judge responds Idk.
  • Proxy Outputs: Intermediate or surrogate responses generated by a model when responding to evaluation prompts.

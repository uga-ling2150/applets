Activity 3E: Understanding RLHF

Learning Objectives

  • Explain how pairwise human preferences become feedback data.
  • Apply helpful, honest, and responsible criteria when comparing responses.
  • Reflect on why human raters may disagree and how their choices influence training.

Background

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) starts with human raters comparing pairs of model responses to the same prompt and recording which one they prefer. Many such comparisons form a preference dataset, which a later stage of training uses to build a reward signal. This classroom simulation uses only prewritten, non-sensitive examples: it does not contact a live AI model, and nothing you select here is stored or submitted anywhere.

Instructions

  1. Read the user request and the two possible assistant responses.
  2. Choose which response you prefer and how strong your preference is.
  3. Select at least one reason that influenced your judgment.
  4. Select Submit Preference, then compare your reasoning with the teaching note.
  5. Complete five comparisons and review your preference record.
  6. You may select Skip This Comparison at any time. Human preference is not the same as objective correctness.

What happens next in real RLHF? Many pairwise comparisons form a preference dataset. Researchers use that dataset to train a preference model, which can later provide a training signal. This activity stops at the human-rating stage.

Reflection & Discussion Guide

  1. Did helpfulness, honesty, and responsibility ever point toward different choices? If so, how did you weigh the different criteria?
  2. Could another reasonable rater have preferred the other response? Why?
  3. What kinds of bias could enter a dataset through human ratings? What might happen if a small group of people with restricted demographics supplies most of the ratings?
  4. What are the pros and cons of using RLHF to guarantee reliability (e.g. factuality, logical soundness) of results?

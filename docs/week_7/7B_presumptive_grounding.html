Activity 7B: Presumptive Grounding

Learning Objectives

  • Identify assumptions and clarification requests in human–AI dialogue.
  • Distinguish AI-initiated clarification from a user's correction after a misunderstanding.
  • Evaluate the benefits and costs of proceeding with an assumption versus asking for clarification.

Background

Conversation partners work together to establish common ground: a shared basis for understanding. A fluent answer can make it look as if this understanding already exists. But did the AI check what you meant, or simply assume? Here, the same language model uses two different sets of instructions. One encourages it to proceed with an interpretation; the other encourages clarification. Neither strategy is always best.

Instructions

  1. Work with a partner. Choose a scenario and agree on what you mean without putting every detail in your opening message.
  2. Send the same opening to both assistants. Predict what each might assume before reading the replies.
  3. Continue each conversation separately. Answer clarification questions or correct misunderstandings. Switch who types and who observes.
  4. Mark useful examples in the transcripts, then explain your comparison in the reflection section. Download your work before leaving.

      How the comparison works

      Both assistants use the same model, temperature and reply-length limit. A is instructed to choose a plausible interpretation and answer; B is instructed to ask a focused question when meaning is unclear, then answer once enough is known. These are tendencies, not guaranteed behaviors. The opening is identical, but later conversation histories can differ.

      Model: Llama 3.1 8B Instruct Fast. The model runs on a hosted service; your computer does not download model weights. A response may vary between runs. This activity illustrates interaction strategies; it does not reproduce the paper's experiment or establish which strategy is universally better.

      Read Shaikh et al. (2024), Grounding Gaps in Language Model Generations (new tab).

      Repair patterns to look for

      Other-initiated self-repair: you say something → the AI asks what you mean → you clarify your own turn.

      Third-position repair: you say something → the AI responds in a way that reveals a misunderstanding → you correct it in your next turn.

      A question that asks for more information is not automatically a clarification request. Ask whether it addresses trouble understanding an earlier turn, or simply moves the task forward.

      Offline discussion example (scripted, not live AI)

      This author-written example is available if the live service is unavailable. It is separate from your live conversations and is not evidence of actual model behavior.

      Proceed with an assumption

      You: Help me prepare for my interview.

      A: Practice describing your work experience and why you want the job.

      You: I mean interviewing my grandfather for an oral-history project.

      A: Prepare open questions about his experiences, and ask permission before recording.

      Ask for clarification

      You: Help me prepare for my interview.

      B: Will you be answering the questions, or interviewing someone else?

      You: I will interview my grandfather for an oral-history project.

      B: Prepare open questions about his experiences, and ask permission before recording.

      Where is the trouble source, and who initiates repair? Which exchange would you prefer, and why?

      Reflection & Discussion Guide

      1. Find a specific assumption or clarification request. What evidence in the preceding conversation supports your interpretation?
      2. Did you need to correct an assistant? Identify the trouble source and who initiated repair.
      3. Which clarification helped? Was any unnecessary or repetitive? Compare the effort of answering questions with the effort of correcting mistakes.
      4. Try the clear-request comparison case. Is clarification useful when the task is already clear?
      5. Why can we not conclude that one strategy is always better from these conversations? Consider prompt instructions, context and variation between runs.

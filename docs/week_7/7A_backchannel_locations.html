Activity 7A: Backchannel Locations

Learning Objectives

  • Identify moments when a listener might offer a brief response while someone else continues speaking.
  • Compare the timing of backchannels across listeners.
  • Explain similarities and differences using the speaker's wording, pauses and intonation.

Background

A backchannel, such as “mm-hmm,” “yeah” or “right,” can show that a listener is following along without taking over the conversation. Here you will listen to a continuous stretch of speech and mark when you would offer such a response. We are comparing listening choices, not searching for a single correct answer.

Instructions

  1. Choose a recording. Use headphones and take turns on one computer. Other participants should avoid hearing the recording or seeing earlier results before their own turn.
  2. The optional practice uses the first 15 seconds of this same recording, so it gives you prior exposure. In a recorded round, imagine listening to the speaker and press Backchannel now whenever you would respond. With that button focused, you can also use Space or Enter.
  3. Listen to the end to save your round. Hand over to the next participant without revealing the comparison.
  4. After everyone has finished, reveal the waveform and markers. Replay selected moments and discuss your choices. Download the results before leaving.

When would you respond?

Listen independently. Mark your moments. Compare together.

    Reflection & Discussion Guide

    1. Find a moment where several listeners marked a backchannel. What was happening in the speaker's wording, pauses or intonation?
    2. Find a moment where your choices differed. How might you explain that difference?
    3. Would your response encourage the speaker to continue, show understanding, or express agreement? Are these always the same thing?
    4. How might reading the transcript, hearing the clip before, or seeing others' choices change your response?
    5. What can these results tell us about regularities in listening, and what can they not establish?

    Your reflection is included in your JSON download. Importing other groups' files adds their marker records, not their written reflections. This page does not submit work to your instructor.

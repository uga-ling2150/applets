Activity 7A: Backchannel Locations

Learning Objectives

  • Identify possible backchannel locations.
  • Compare response timing across listeners.
  • Explain patterns using wording, pauses and intonation.

Background

Backchannels such as “mm-hmm” or “yeah” acknowledge a speaker without taking over. Mark when you would respond, then compare with your class. There is no single correct answer.

Instructions

  1. Choose the same recording as your class, put on headphones, and press Start listening.
  2. Click I would respond here each time you would say “mm-hmm” or “yeah”. You can also use Space or Enter while that button is focused.
  3. Listen to the end. Your clicks are saved automatically and appear on a timeline. Compare with your class when your teacher shares the results.

      What is saved?

      Your browser generates a random key for each activity; the server assigns its P number. No student enters an ID. This identifies a browser record, not a verified student. Class activities save each attempt, task actions (including reloads, pauses and markers), client-reported times and server receipt/submission times. Only the teacher can access the action/time history; the shared comparison uses the first completed attempt per participant. Classroom records contain no student names, emails, IP addresses, device fingerprints, voices or written reflections. The hosting provider processes ordinary network requests. Timestamps and receipts are pseudonymous and can identify a record when combined with information supplied by a student. A student can give their receipt (collection reference + participant + attempt) to the teacher to locate a record. Clearing browser storage or changing devices creates a new anonymous participant. Network interruptions can delay syncing; closing or clearing storage before sync can lose pending actions. Up to 20 attempts per participant and 20,000 task events per activity are saved (up to 1,000 per attempt or participant-level log). Class records expire after 30 days. Outside class mode, individual practice stays in your browser; downloads provide a backup. Internet is required for class submission.

Activity 9A: Turn-taking and TRPs

Learning Objectives

  • Identify possible transition relevance places (TRPs) while listening to natural conversation.
  • Explain how grammar, intonation and the action being performed shape expectations about a turn ending.
  • Compare your timing judgments with observed transitions in the recordings and published descriptions of English conversation.

Background

A TRP is a point where a speaker's turn could be complete and another speaker could take the floor. There can be more than one possible point in a turn. Conversation partners use more than silence: wording, intonation and context help them anticipate when to enter.

Instructions

  1. Listen to each of five short meeting excerpts. Words appear as the speaker says them.
  2. Press I would start speaking now once when you think you could take the next turn. You can use Space or Enter while that button has focus. The excerpt keeps playing after your choice.
  3. After all five, compare your click positions with the annotated turn endings. Discuss why a different point might also have been possible.

Compare with English conversation

In Levinson and Torreira's analysis of the English Switchboard telephone corpus, 30.1% of floor transfers involved speakers talking at the same time; the remaining 69.9% involved a gap. Their paper shows the full distribution of actual floor-transfer offsets in 100 ms bins. These are speech onsets in conversations, whereas the chart above contains interface clicks relative to selected AMI turn endings. Compare the shape and timing questions in discussion, but do not treat the percentages or bins as equivalent measures.

Reflection & Discussion Guide

  1. Which words, pauses or intonation patterns made a turn seem possibly complete?
  2. Did any speaker continue after a point where you could have entered? What changed your expectation?
  3. Why would your click time differ from an actual next speaker's speech onset?

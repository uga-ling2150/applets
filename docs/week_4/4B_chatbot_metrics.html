Activity 4B: Chatbot Metrics Tug-of-War

Learning objectives

After this activity, you will be able to:

  • Explain containment, task completion, and abandonment rates.
  • Predict how an automatic handoff rule changes the three metrics.
  • Evaluate why one business metric cannot stand in for a good customer experience.

Background

Organizations use business metrics to judge whether a chatbot reduces costs and helps customers. Containment measures how often the chatbot avoids a human handoff. Task completion measures whether the customer eventually gets the task done. Abandonment measures how often the customer gives up before either kind of help succeeds.

These goals can pull in different directions. A chatbot that waits longer before transferring a call may contain more calls, but some customers may leave while they wait. An early transfer can prevent abandonment and protect task completion, but it lowers containment.

Reflection & Discussion Guide

  • Which handoff setting would you choose if the company cared most about containment? Who bears the cost of that choice?
  • Which setting best protects task completion and reduces abandonment? What business cost might increase?
  • Containment is widely used in practice. How could targeting it encourage a chatbot to avoid a necessary handoff or keep doing the wrong thing?
  • What other evidence would you want before deciding that one handoff policy creates a better customer experience?

Closing Note

Changing one operational rule can improve one metric while worsening another. This is one example of Goodhart's Law: when an organization turns a measure into a target, people and systems may optimize the number without improving the underlying experience. A useful evaluation therefore needs several measures and careful interpretation.
